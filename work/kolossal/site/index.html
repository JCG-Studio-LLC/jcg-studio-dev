01 · Sunlight zone · 0 to 200 m

We go big to explore & protect our oceans.

KOLOSSAL is an ocean exploration and conservation nonprofit in Venice, CA. Situated at the nexus of cutting-edge technology, marine conservation, and ambitious ocean exploration that would hopefully make Jacques Cousteau smile, KOLOSSAL contains multitudes. And we’re proud of it.

We are working on high-profile expeditions to find and film exotic sea creatures in their natural habitats, shining light on these amazing (and rarely seen) species to inspire excitement and pique the public’s interest.

95% of our oceans remain unexplored, which is about 95% too much for us.

02 · Twilight zone · 200 to 1,000 m

What do you get when a scientist, a salesman, an accountant, a screenwriter, a speech pathologist, and a research analyst walk into a bar?

When they all happen to be friends, and when they all have a shared passion for the ocean, you get KOLOSSAL. Established in 2016, we envisioned an environmental organization whose primary message was based in optimism and hope to inspire the world to protect our most valuable resource: the ocean.

Ambitious but with a grassroots mentality, we want to prove that ocean exploration isn’t exclusively reserved for the select few.

03 · The expedition

Let’s get kraken.

The barriers to deep-sea research have been historically high. KOLOSSAL wants to change that: we want to use this expedition to develop easy and low-cost deep-sea research platforms that can be readily shipped in a research “toolkit” and easily deployed by volunteers on any seagoing vessel.

The centerpiece technology is an innovative deep-sea platform called the Autonomous Camera for Kraken Baiting and Recording, or ACKBAR, for short. And yes, in reference to that ACKBAR.

04 · Conservation campaign

Take a pass on Chilean sea bass.

Southern Ocean fishing has been controversial since Patagonian toothfish was rebranded as “Chilean sea bass” in the 1990s and exploded in popularity. The rush to harvest this “white gold” devastated one of Earth’s last pristine marine ecosystems, home to the colossal squid.

Our organization is working to remove Chilean sea bass from menus for good. We partner with restaurants and retailers to promote local, sustainable alternatives, and encourage consumers to make environmentally-conscious seafood choices.

05 · Midnight zone · below 1,000 m

In the deep sea off of Antarctica is the colossal squid.

While the colossal squid is likely prehistoric, it wasn’t until 1925 that humans knew of its existence. Fishermen discovered two colossal squid arms in the stomach of a sperm whale, and thus birthed the species Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni.

Because of the mystery surrounding this enormous creature, the colossal squid is the perfect animal to ignite the public imagination and inspire people to explore and protect the deep sea.
